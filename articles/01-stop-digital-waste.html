1. Why Traditional Note-Taking Apps Fail to Compound

Albert Einstein famously reportedly called compound interest the eighth wonder of the world: "He who understands it, earns it; he who doesn't, pays it."

In personal knowledge management (PKM), true knowledge compounding happens when an idea you jot down today automatically collides and connects with notes you wrote three months, a year, or three years ago—sparking an entirely new synthesis or decision framework.

Yet traditional note-taking apps kill compounding at the architectural level:

1) Rigid Folder Hierarchies Create Information Silos

Folders force you into premature categorization. Where do you file a note about "Applying Bayesian Updating to Value Investing"? Under AI & Math or Finance & Investing? Whichever folder you choose, you sever that thought from the other discipline. Biological neurons work as a dense synaptic network—folders lock your ideas into isolated drawers.

2) Cloud Lock-In and AI Training Anxiety

Your rawest thoughts, proprietary architectures, and private journals sit inside someone else's proprietary cloud database. If the vendor hikes subscription prices, changes its terms, or goes offline, your intellectual assets are held hostage. Worse yet, in the age of LLMs, cloud-hosted notes risk becoming free training data for opaque corporate AI models.

3) The Illusion of Productivity ("Hoarding Without Thinking")

Most apps act as passive storage lockers. They lack a catalytic engine to challenge your assumptions or turn scattered sparks into rigorous mental models. You can hoard 1,000 highlights and still draw a blank when facing a high-stakes decision.
